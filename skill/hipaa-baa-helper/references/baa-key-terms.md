# BAA key terms

Use this after a "Yes, a BAA is required" determination, or when the user asks directly what a BAA should say. It is the source for a key terms sheet, not a full contract. Every authority below was checked against the text of the regulation. Do not add a citation that is not in this file without verifying it.

## Part 1: terms the regulation requires

A BAA that leaves these out is deficient. The source is 45 CFR § 164.504(e)(2) and (e)(4), 45 CFR § 164.314(a)(2)(i) for the Security Rule, and 45 CFR § 164.410 for breach notice. The sample language is a starting point in plain contract English. Replace the bracketed names.

| # | Term | What it must do | Authority |
|---|------|-----------------|-----------|
| 1 | Permitted and required uses and disclosures | State what the business associate may do with PHI, tied to the services. Cannot authorize a use the covered entity itself could not make. | § 164.504(e)(2)(i) |
| 2 | Management and administration | May permit the business associate to use and disclose PHI for its own proper management and administration and to carry out its legal responsibilities. Disclosures for those purposes need either a legal requirement or reasonable assurances of confidentiality plus notice of any breach by the recipient. | § 164.504(e)(2)(i)(A), (e)(4) |
| 3 | Data aggregation | Optional. May permit data aggregation services relating to the covered entity's health care operations. Include only if the services actually involve it. | § 164.504(e)(2)(i)(B) |
| 4 | No other use or disclosure | Business associate will not use or further disclose PHI other than as the contract permits or the law requires. | § 164.504(e)(2)(ii)(A) |
| 5 | Safeguards and Security Rule | Use appropriate safeguards and comply with the Security Rule for electronic PHI. | § 164.504(e)(2)(ii)(B); § 164.314(a)(2)(i)(A) |
| 6 | Reporting | Report to the covered entity any use or disclosure not provided for by the contract, including breaches of unsecured PHI, and report any security incident it becomes aware of. | § 164.504(e)(2)(ii)(C); § 164.314(a)(2)(i)(C) |
| 7 | Breach notification | Notify the covered entity of a breach of unsecured PHI without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 calendar days after discovery, with the identities of affected individuals and the other information the covered entity needs for its own notices. The contract may set a shorter deadline. | § 164.410(a)-(c) |
| 8 | Subcontractor flow-down | Ensure every subcontractor that handles PHI agrees to the same restrictions and conditions, and, for electronic PHI, to comply with the Security Rule through its own written contract. | § 164.504(e)(2)(ii)(D); § 164.502(e)(1)(ii); § 164.314(a)(2)(i)(B) |
| 9 | Individual access | Make PHI available so the covered entity can meet access requests. | § 164.504(e)(2)(ii)(E); § 164.524 |
| 10 | Amendment | Make PHI available for amendment and incorporate amendments. | § 164.504(e)(2)(ii)(F); § 164.526 |
| 11 | Accounting of disclosures | Make available the information needed for an accounting of disclosures. | § 164.504(e)(2)(ii)(G); § 164.528 |
| 12 | Carrying out the covered entity's duties | If the business associate performs one of the covered entity's Privacy Rule obligations, it must comply with the rules that apply to the covered entity for that obligation. | § 164.504(e)(2)(ii)(H) |
| 13 | Books and records to HHS | Make internal practices, books, and records on PHI available to the Secretary for compliance review. | § 164.504(e)(2)(ii)(I) |
| 14 | Return or destroy at termination | At termination, if feasible, return or destroy all PHI and keep no copies. If not feasible, extend the contract's protections to the PHI and limit further use to the purposes that make return or destruction infeasible. | § 164.504(e)(2)(ii)(J) |
| 15 | Termination for material breach | Authorize the covered entity to terminate if the business associate violates a material term. | § 164.504(e)(2)(iii) |
| 16 | Written contract | The assurances must be documented in a written contract that meets § 164.504(e). A subcontractor agreement must meet the same requirements. | § 164.502(e)(2); § 164.314(a)(2)(iii) |

Sample language for the terms users most often ask about:

- Uses (1, 4): "[Business Associate] may use and disclose Protected Health Information only to perform the Services described in the [Services Agreement], as permitted by this Agreement, or as Required by Law. [Business Associate] shall not use or disclose PHI in a manner that would violate the HIPAA Privacy Rule if done by [Covered Entity]."
- Reporting (6): "[Business Associate] shall report to [Covered Entity] any use or disclosure of PHI not provided for by this Agreement, and any Security Incident, of which it becomes aware, including any Breach of Unsecured PHI."
- Breach notice (7): "[Business Associate] shall notify [Covered Entity] of a Breach of Unsecured PHI without unreasonable delay and in no case later than [X] calendar days after discovery, and shall provide the information [Covered Entity] needs to make notifications under 45 CFR Part 164, Subpart D."
- Subcontractors (8): "[Business Associate] shall ensure that any subcontractor that creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on its behalf agrees in a written contract to the same restrictions, conditions, and requirements that apply to [Business Associate] under this Agreement."
- Return or destroy (14): "On termination, [Business Associate] shall, if feasible, return or destroy all PHI and retain no copies. If return or destruction is not feasible, [Business Associate] shall extend the protections of this Agreement to the PHI and limit further uses and disclosures to the purposes that make return or destruction infeasible."

## Part 2: terms to negotiate

The regulation does not set these. They are business and risk decisions, and the right position depends on which seat the user holds. Give a recommendation for the user's side, say why in one line, and mark it as a negotiating position, not a legal requirement.

| Term | Covered entity (or upstream party) usually wants | Business associate (or downstream vendor) usually wants |
|------|---------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| Breach notice window | Short and fixed, often 5 to 30 days. The 60-day outer limit is a ceiling, not a target. A business associate that acts as the covered entity's agent can start the covered entity's own clock when it discovers a breach, which is why covered entities press for short windows. | The 60-day outer limit, or a middle figure it can actually meet, measured from discovery. |
| Security incident reporting | Report all security incidents. | Define "unsuccessful security incidents" (pings, port scans, blocked log-in attempts) as reported by this paragraph, with no further notice. |
| Cost of breach response | Business associate pays for notices, credit monitoring, and investigation when it caused the breach. | Cap and tie costs to its own fault and to reasonable, documented expenses. |
| Indemnity, liability cap, insurance | Uncapped or super-capped liability for BAA breaches, cyber insurance with named minimums. | Fold BAA liability into the services agreement cap, or a separate agreed super-cap. |
| Audit and inspection | Right to audit safeguards, or to receive a third-party report (SOC 2, HITRUST). | Report-in-lieu-of-audit, reasonable notice, once a year, at the covered entity's cost. |
| Subcontractors and offshore | Prior notice or approval for new subcontractors, and no offshore handling of PHI without consent. | Blanket approval for named subcontractors, notice only for material changes. |
| De-identification rights | Business associate may not de-identify PHI or use it for its own purposes. De-identifying is itself a use of PHI, so the contract must allow it (see § 164.502(d)). | Express right to de-identify PHI and use the result, if the product depends on it. |
| Cure period before termination | Short or none for material breach. | A cure period (such as 30 days) before the covered entity may terminate. |
| Precedence | The BAA controls over the services agreement on any PHI conflict. | Same, usually acceptable, but check for conflicts with limits of liability. |
| Governing law and state law | The covered entity's state, with the stricter of HIPAA and state privacy law applying. | A neutral or its own state. |

Two background points to mention when relevant:

- Since the HITECH Act and the 2013 Omnibus Rule, business associates are directly regulated for the Security Rule, for impermissible uses and disclosures, and for breach notification. The BAA is not the only source of their duties.
- A BAA usually attaches to an underlying services agreement. Ask for its name and date, and add a clause saying the BAA controls over it for anything involving PHI.

## Part 3: gathering facts and shaping the output

Ask only what you do not already know, in one short batch, and use bracketed placeholders for anything the user cannot answer yet. Do not hold up a useful draft over missing details.

1. Which seat is the user in: the covered entity hiring a vendor, a business associate hiring a subcontractor, or the vendor being asked to sign? The classification answers usually settle this. If the user is a business associate, the agreement is a subcontractor BAA, and the "covered entity" role in the sample language becomes the upstream business associate.
2. Names of both parties, and the name and date of the underlying services agreement.
3. What the services are, and which kinds of PHI are involved.
4. Whether subcontractors will be used, and whether any PHI will be handled offshore.
5. Any preference on breach notice window, return or destruction, and governing law.

Produce this structure:

**Key terms sheet: BAA between [Party A] and [Party B]**

1. **Parties and roles.** Who is the covered entity or upstream party, who is the business associate or downstream party, and which one the user is.
2. **Required terms.** The terms from Part 1 that apply, each with the plain-English requirement, the authority, and sample language where Part 1 gives it. Leave out term 3 (data aggregation) unless it fits the services.
3. **Terms to negotiate.** The relevant rows from Part 2, each with a recommended position for the user's seat and a one-line reason.
4. **Open points and assumptions.** What you assumed, what is still a placeholder, and anything that needs the other side's input.

End with the not-legal-advice reminder and say that this is a key terms sheet, not an executed-ready agreement. Offer to expand it into a full draft or a Word document if the user wants one, and say a lawyer should review any agreement before signature. Do not offer to send, sign, or submit anything.
